Tips for Staying Secure

1
Never reuse passwords. Each account should have a unique password — if one is compromised, others stay safe.
2
Use a password manager. Tools like Bitwarden or 1Password securely store all your passwords so you only need to remember one master password.
3
Enable two-factor authentication (2FA). Adding a second verification step — like a code sent to your phone — dramatically increases security.
4
Never share your password. Not even with IT staff or teachers. Legitimate services will never ask for your password.
5
Avoid personal information. Don't use your name, birthdate, school name, or pet's name — these are easily guessed by attackers.
6
Watch out for phishing. Be suspicious of emails or messages asking you to click a link and log in — always navigate directly to websites.
7
Change passwords if compromised. If you suspect a breach, change your password immediately and check haveibeenpwned.com to see if your email was exposed.
8
Lock your screen when stepping away from your device — even for a moment. On most devices: Windows key + L or Ctrl + Command + Q.
9
Use passphrases for memorable security. A phrase like "BlueDingo2022!" is easier to remember than random characters, yet just as secure.
10
Longer is stronger. Every additional character exponentially increases the number of combinations an attacker must try.
